# Top Research Questions for India Time Use Survey 2024

**Date**: November 8, 2025
**Source**: Analysis of Master Descriptive Statistics

---

## Tier 1: Highly Novel, Strong Data, High Impact

### 1. The Digital Transformation of Social Life
**Motivating Data Pattern**: Remote socializing (Code 82) is far more common (1.55% participation) than in-person socializing (Code 81, 0.04% participation).
**Research Question**: How do gender, marital status, and age influence the choice between remote and in-person socializing? Is there a "digital social penalty" for married women?
**Novelty**: This question challenges traditional assumptions about community life in India and captures the societal shift toward digital interaction.
**Feasibility**: **High**. The participation rate for remote socializing is sufficient for robust analysis, including interaction effects.

### 2. The Widowhood Exercise Dividend
**Motivating Data Pattern**: Widowed individuals, particularly women, report higher participation in exercise (9.74% for women, 10.67% for men) than their married counterparts (6.95% for women, 9.00% for men).
**Research Question**: Why does widowhood appear to increase time spent on exercise? Is this due to freedom from spousal care, increased health consciousness, or other factors?
**Novelty**: This finding directly contradicts the "marriage penalty" narrative and suggests that marriage may constrain self-care activities.
**Feasibility**: **High**. Exercise participation is high overall (7.51%), and sample sizes for widowed individuals are large enough for meaningful analysis.

### 3. The "Ceremony Tax": Quantifying Social Obligations
**Motivating Data Pattern**: A significant portion of the population (7.13%) participates in ceremonies (Code 84), which is higher than participation in exercise.
**Research Question**: Are there gendered differences in the obligation to attend ceremonies? How do these social obligations vary by marital status and age?
**Novelty**: This research quantifies the time cost of social obligations, a culturally significant but understudied aspect of time use.
**Feasibility**: **Very High**. The high participation rate allows for detailed demographic breakdowns.

## Tier 2: Strong Findings, Good Data

### 4. The 3.7-Hour Beauty Burden
**Motivating Data Pattern**: Time spent on personal hygiene (Code 91) averages 222 minutes (3.7 hours) among participants, with a 13.27% participation rate.
**Research Question**: Is there a significant gender gap in time spent on personal hygiene, reflecting different societal standards for grooming?
**Novelty**: This question investigates "beauty labor" as a quantifiable time burden, an area of growing interest in gender studies.
**Feasibility**: **Very High**. The high participation rate ensures a large sample for analysis.

### 5. The Retirement Exercise Dividend
**Motivating Data Pattern**: Older age groups (65+) show higher rates of exercise participation (11-12%) than younger adults (18-24, 7-8%).
**Research Question**: Does retirement free up time for exercise, or does this pattern reflect a survivor bias where healthier individuals live longer?
**Novelty**: This counter-intuitive finding challenges typical narratives about age and health decline.
**Feasibility**: **Very High**. Participation rates are strong across all age groups.

## Data Quality Issues and Recommendations

### 1. Zero Participation in Religious Activities
**Observation**: The data shows 0% participation in religious activities (Code 9) across all demographics. This is highly improbable and suggests a data quality issue.
**Recommendation**: Investigate whether religious activities have been miscoded, possibly under "ceremonies" (Code 84) or another category. A review of the data dictionary is essential before proceeding with any analysis of religious time use.

### 2. Low Participation in Shopping
**Observation**: Participation in shopping (Code 34) is surprisingly low (1.62%).
**Recommendation**: This may be a measurement issue. Short trips may not be logged, or shopping might be bundled with other activities. This requires further investigation.
