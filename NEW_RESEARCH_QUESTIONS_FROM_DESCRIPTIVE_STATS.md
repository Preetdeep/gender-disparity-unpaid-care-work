# New Research Questions from Master Descriptive Statistics Analysis

**Date**: November 8, 2025

## Top 10 New Research Questions

### 1. **The Great Digital Disconnect: Why India Socializes Remotely, Not In-Person**

**Data Pattern**: Remote socializing (Code 82) is **39 times more common** than in-person socializing (Code 81)
- In-person socializing (Code 81): 0.04% participation (104.42 min when participating)
- Remote socializing (Code 82): 1.55% participation (86.39 min when participating)

**Research Questions**:
- How do gender, marital status, and age affect the choice between remote vs in-person socializing?
- Does marriage push people toward remote socializing over face-to-face friendships?
- What are the urban/rural differences in digital vs physical social networks?
- Is there a "digital social penalty" for married women?

**Why Novel**: Challenges assumptions about "traditional" Indian family/community life; captures post-pandemic shift to digital socialization

**Feasibility**: HIGH - Good participation rates for remote socializing (1.55%), sufficient sample for gender × marital status interactions

**JFE-Style Title**: *"Digital Distance: The Shift from In-Person to Remote Socializing in India"*

---

### 2. **The Widowhood Exercise Boom**

**Data Pattern**: Widowed people exercise **MORE** than married people!
- Widowed women: 9.74% participation, 60.57 min average
- Married women: 6.95% participation, 54.29 min average
- Widowed men: 10.67% participation, 65.31 min average
- Married men: 9.00% participation, 54.51 min average

**Research Questions**:
- Why does widowhood increase exercise participation and time?
- Is this about freedom from spousal care duties?
- Health consciousness after loss of partner?
- Gender differences in the widowhood effect on self-care?

**Why Novel**: Completely contradicts the "marriage penalty" narrative - shows marriage constrains exercise MORE than widowhood

**Feasibility**: HIGH - 7.51% overall exercise participation, large samples for widowed populations

**JFE-Style Title**: *"The Widowhood Health Dividend: Exercise and Self-Care After Spousal Loss"*

---

### 3. **The 3.7-Hour Hygiene Burden**

**Data Pattern**: Personal hygiene (Code 91) takes a whopping **222 minutes (3.7 hours)** on average when participating!
- 13.27% participation rate
- Median: 180 minutes (3 hours)

**Research Questions**:
- Is there a massive gender gap driven by beauty standards?
- How does the hygiene time burden vary by marital status (married women doing more)?
- Rural vs urban differences in grooming expectations?
- What counts as "hygiene" - bathing, grooming, makeup, hair care?

**Why Interesting**: Could reveal gendered beauty labor and differential grooming standards

**Feasibility**: VERY HIGH - 13.27% participation, second-highest activity after eating

**JFE-Style Title**: *"The Beauty Burden: Gender Gaps in Personal Hygiene and Grooming Time"*

---

### 4. **Ceremonies: India's Time-Consuming Social Obligation**

**Data Pattern**: 7.13% participate in ceremonies (Code 84) - **higher than exercise**!
- Mean time: 60.50 minutes
- 60,849 people in sample

**Research Questions**:
- Are women obligated to attend more ceremonies than men?
- Class differences in ceremonial obligations?
- Marriage penalty in ceremony attendance (married women attend more)?
- Age gradient (do older people attend more ceremonies)?

**Why Fascinating**: Quantifies the "social obligation" burden; could show gendered expectations for family/community events

**Feasibility**: VERY HIGH - 7.13% participation, robust sample

**JFE-Style Title**: *"The Ceremony Tax: Gendered Social Obligations and Time Use in India"*

---

### 5. **The Sports Consumption vs Participation Gap**

**Data Pattern**:
- Watching sports events (Code 74): 1.89% participation, 40.27 min average
- Playing sports/exercise (Code 71): 7.51% participation, 54.73 min average

**Research Questions**:
- Do men watch more sports while women play less (or vice versa)?
- Class gradient in passive vs active sports engagement?
- Is sports watching a form of male leisure protected from household duties?
- Urban vs rural differences in sports consumption vs participation?

**Why Interesting**: Passive leisure (watching) vs active leisure (playing) - different class/gender patterns

**Feasibility**: HIGH - Good participation rates for both activities

**JFE-Style Title**: *"Spectators and Players: Gender and Class Gaps in Sports Engagement"*

---

### 6. **The Nap Class Gradient**

**Data Pattern**: 10.74% engage in sleeping/napping during the day (Code 93)
- Mean: 34.25 minutes
- 89,778 people

**Research Questions**:
- Who has the privilege to nap? (Retirees, wealthy, or exhausted workers?)
- Gender differences (women napping with babies vs men's leisure naps)?
- Urban vs rural nap culture?
- Education/class gradient in daytime sleep?

**Why Fun**: Could be titled "Naps and Inequality" - reveals who can afford rest during the day

**Feasibility**: VERY HIGH - 10.74% participation

**JFE-Style Title**: *"Naps and Inequality: Who Gets to Sleep During the Day in India?"*

---

### 7. **The Vanishing Library: Education Infrastructure and Access**

**Data Pattern**: Only 0.48% use library/learning resources (Code 24)
- Mean: 47.14 minutes
- 3,801 people

**Research Questions**:
- Urban vs rural access disparities?
- Gender differences in library usage?
- Is this lack of infrastructure or cultural factors?
- Education level gradient in library access?

**Why Important**: Policy relevant for education investment and infrastructure

**Feasibility**: MODERATE - Low participation (0.48%) but still 3,801 observations

**JFE-Style Title**: *"The Library Desert: Educational Infrastructure Access in India"*

---

### 8. **The Teaching Children Vacuum**

**Data Pattern**: Only 0.05% spend time teaching/supervising children's education (Code 42)
- Mean: 45.14 minutes
- Only 437 people

**Research Questions**:
- Gender gap in educational engagement with children?
- Class differences (wealthy hire tutors, poor parents teach)?
- Household head gender impact on parental teaching?
- Why is this SO low compared to physical childcare (3.09%)?

**Why Critical**: Challenges "helicopter parenting" narratives; shows parental educational engagement is rare

**Feasibility**: LOW - Only 0.05% participation (437 people)

**Recommendation**: Combine with other educational activities (Codes 21-25) for broader "learning time" measure

---

### 9. **Healthcare Seeking Behavior and Gender**

**Data Pattern**: Only 0.19% engaged in healthcare activities (Code 94)
- Mean: 46.32 minutes
- 1,557 people

**Research Questions**:
- Do women seek healthcare less (putting family first)?
- Rural vs urban healthcare access?
- Age gradient in healthcare seeking?
- Gender of household head effect on family healthcare?

**Why Important**: Healthcare access and gender discrimination in health-seeking

**Feasibility**: LOW - Only 0.19% participation

**Recommendation**: This may be a measurement issue (only captures time spent seeking care on survey day, not frequency)

---

### 10. **The Reverse Age Exercise Paradox**

**Data Pattern**: Older people exercise MORE than younger people!
- Age 65+: 11-12% participation
- Age 18-24: 7-8% participation
- Mean time similar across ages (~54-64 min)

**Research Questions**:
- Are retirees healthier or do they have more time?
- Is this survivor bias (healthy people live longer)?
- Work constraints preventing young adults from exercising?
- Gender differences in the age-exercise gradient?

**Why Surprising**: Contradicts typical age-health decline patterns

**Feasibility**: VERY HIGH - Strong participation across all age groups

**JFE-Style Title**: *"The Retirement Exercise Dividend: Age, Work, and Physical Activity"*

## Data Quality Notes

### Concern: Religious Activities (Code 9)
- Shows **0% participation** across ALL demographics
- This seems incorrect - either:
  - Religious activities coded under different codes (ceremonies?)
  - Data quality issue
  - Religious time use not captured as "major activity"
- **Recommendation**: Investigate data dictionary and cross-check with ceremony attendance

### Shopping Puzzle
- Only 1.62% participation (Code 34) - surprisingly low for daily activity
- May be measurement issue (quick trips not logged, or shopping bundled with other activities)
- Worth investigating gender differences despite low participation
