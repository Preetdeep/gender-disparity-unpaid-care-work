I submit "Who Cares When Care Is Needed? Childcare, Dependent-Adult Care, and Gender in Indian Households" for consideration in *Feminist Economics*.

India's Time Use Survey 2024 asks each household whether a member needs special care and whether a caregiver is available. This makes it possible to measure the gender division of care conditional on actual need, rather than mixing it with who lives with whom. In households with a member needing special care, women provide 76% of dependent-adult care minutes. Need raises women's adult-care time by 37 minutes a day and men's by 16. Long-term care policy in India is, in practice, policy about women's time.

Companion papers: this manuscript is one of four papers I am submitting from the India Time Use Survey 2024 microdata, each to a different journal and each on a distinct question: a paper on the time-use correlates of marriage for women and men (submitted to the *Journal of Marriage and Family*); a paper comparing the time use of sisters and brothers (submitted to the *Journal of Development Studies*); a paper on how salaried men use the time released on Sundays (submitted to the *Journal of Time Use Research*).
