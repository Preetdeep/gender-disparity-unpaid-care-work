# Paper 03: Who Cares When Care Is Needed?

**Target journal:** *Feminist Economics*. Its core subject is the measurement and gendering of care, and it publishes Indian time-use work (Hirway and Jose 2011).
**Fallbacks:** *Population and Development Review*, *Journal of Aging & Social Policy*, *Ageing & Society*.

## Contribution in one line

Conditioning on the survey's own care-need questions separates the gender division of care from household composition. It also gives the first diary-based estimate of how much dependent-adult care Indian women provide, and at what cost.

## Cover letter (draft)

> Dear Editors,
>
> I submit "Who Cares When Care Is Needed? Childcare, Dependent-Adult Care, and Gender in Indian Households" for consideration in *Feminist Economics*.
>
> Population averages of care time mix the gender division of care with differences in who lives with someone who needs care. India's Time Use Survey 2024 asks each household whether a member needs special care and whether a caregiver is available, and records diaries for every member. This lets me compare childcare and dependent-adult care in the households that need each, measure women's share of each, and estimate the cost of adult-care need in women's paid work, leisure and sleep. With India ageing quickly and formal long-term care minimal, the results describe who will absorb the coming growth in care demand.
