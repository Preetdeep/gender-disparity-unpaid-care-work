Dear Editor,

I submit "Daughters' Days: Son Preference, Gender Norms and the Time Use of Sisters and Brothers in India" for consideration in the *Journal of Development Studies*.

Son preference in India is well documented in sex ratios, health and nutrition, but much less in how households allocate children's own time. Using 24-hour diaries from India's Time Use Survey 2024 and sibling-set fixed effects (34,133 children in 13,984 mixed-sex sets), I find that girls aged 6–17 do about 41 more minutes of unpaid domestic work a day than their brothers and have about 34 fewer minutes of free time. The gap is positive and significant in all 13 alternative specifications (21–61 minutes), including two-child sets and sets whose eldest child is a boy, so fertility-stopping selection explains at most part of it.

Companion papers: this manuscript is one of four papers I am submitting from the India Time Use Survey 2024 microdata, each to a different journal and each on a distinct question: a paper on who provides care in households with a member who needs care (submitted to *Feminist Economics*); a paper on the time-use correlates of marriage for women and men (submitted to the *Journal of Marriage and Family*); a paper on how salaried men use the time released on Sundays (submitted to the *Journal of Time Use Research*). The papers share the data-processing code and the description of the survey, but not their research questions, samples, estimates or conclusions.
