# Paper 06: Daughters' Days

**Title:** Daughters' Days: Son Preference, Gender Norms and the Time Use of Sisters and Brothers in India
**Target journal:** *Journal of Development Studies*. It publishes empirical work on gender discrimination within Indian households and on child labour and schooling (e.g. Das Gupta et al. 2003; Kingdon 2002; Dhar, Jain and Jayachandran 2019 appeared there).
**Fallbacks:** *Economic Development and Cultural Change*, *Population and Development Review*, *World Development*.

## Contribution in one line

Diaries for sisters and brothers in the same household, with sibling-set fixed effects, measure gender discrimination in the allocation of children's own time (domestic work, care, study, leisure), trace the full 1,440-minute reallocation, and test birth-order and fertility-stopping explanations.

## Design (as implemented in `manuscript.Rmd`)

- Sample: never-married children or grandchildren of the head aged 6-17; sibling set = same relationship code in the same household; FE sample = mixed-sex sets.
- Main: `y ~ girl + i(age) | sibling set`, survey-weighted, FSU-clustered. Unaffected by the missing household questionnaire (the set FE absorbs all household characteristics).
- Cross-sectional comparison (all children 6-17): SES proxies (head's education, highest education among adults 18+ built in the Rmd, any regular wage/salaried member), roster-based household size and under-6 count, proxy report; district + day-of-week (+ sub-round if present) FE. `hh_n_employed` deliberately not used (counts the child's/mother's own employment, an outcome).
- Time accounting: girl gap in all nine ICATUS divisions (sums to zero); task breakdown (food, cleaning, laundry, shopping, childcare, water/fuel).
- Age profile: `i(age_grp, girl)` in one regression on all mixed sets (replaces the earlier by-age subsamples, which dropped sets whose siblings fell in different age groups).
- Birth order: age-rank indicators; girl x eldest interaction.
- Heterogeneity (girl x moderator): urban, any regular wage/salaried member (SES proxy; replaces top-2 consumption quintiles), mother's education (mother linked in Rmd), head's education, child under six, Sunday diary.
- Fertility-stopping and robustness: two-child sets, eldest-is-boy vs eldest-is-girl sets, head's children only, no co-resident sibling 18+, self-reported diaries, Mon-Sat vs Sunday, day-of-week indicators, unweighted, normal days (`type_day`, only if present).
- Mothers (secondary, no FE): all-daughters indicator; adolescent daughters vs sons on mother's domestic work. Controls: SES proxies (head's education, any regular member), household size, under-6 count; district + day-of-week (+ sub-round) FE.
- Regional (north/south, or high/low sex-ratio states) heterogeneity is **not** done: no official 2024 state-code list is in `docs/` (the 2019 design note lists state names only, without codes, and boundaries changed after 2019: J&K/Ladakh, DNH&DD). Add it only if an official state-code list is obtained.
- Data disclosure: `r data_disclosure(V)` is in the Data section. Limitations state the absence of caste, religion and consumption (son preference varies by these).

## Not estimable with the 2024 person-level file

- Heterogeneity by consumption quintile, caste/social group or religion; controls for them in the cross-sectional and mothers' models (proxied by education, regular employment and district FE).
- Regional split by state (no reliable state-code mapping).

## Cover letter (draft)

> Dear Editor,
>
> I submit "Daughters' Days: Son Preference, Gender Norms and the Time Use of Sisters and Brothers in India" for consideration in the *Journal of Development Studies*.
>
> Son preference in India is documented in sex ratios, mortality, nutrition, health care and breastfeeding. This paper asks whether it extends to the use of children's own time. India's Time Use Survey 2024 records diaries for every household member aged six and above. Comparing sisters and brothers aged 6 to 17 within the same household removes all family-level confounders. Because each diary covers the whole day, the paper traces exactly which activities girls' extra domestic work displaces, documents when the gap opens over adolescence and by birth order, and tests whether the selection created by son-biased fertility stopping can account for it.
>
> The manuscript is original and not under review elsewhere. Replication code is public; the microdata are available from the National Statistics Office, Government of India.
>
> Sincerely,
> Preet Deep Singh
