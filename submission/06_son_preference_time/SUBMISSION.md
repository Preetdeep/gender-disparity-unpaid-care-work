# Paper 06: Daughters' Days

**Target journal:** *Journal of Development Studies*. It publishes empirical work on gender discrimination within Indian households and on child labour and schooling.
**Fallbacks:** *Economic Development and Cultural Change*, *Population and Development Review*, *World Development*.

## Contribution in one line

Diaries for sisters and brothers in the same household, with sibling-set fixed effects, document son preference in the allocation of children's own time: domestic work, care, study and leisure.

## Cover letter (draft)

> Dear Editor,
>
> I submit "Daughters' Days" for consideration in *Journal of Development Studies*.
>
> Son preference in India is documented in sex ratios, nutrition and health inputs. I show that it also shapes how children's own time is used. India's Time Use Survey 2024 records diaries for every household member aged six and above. Comparing sisters and brothers aged 6 to 17 within the same household removes all family-level confounders and shows how much more domestic and care work girls do, and how much less time they have for learning and leisure, at each age.
>
> The manuscript is original and not under review elsewhere. Replication code is public; the microdata are available from the National Statistics Office, Government of India.
