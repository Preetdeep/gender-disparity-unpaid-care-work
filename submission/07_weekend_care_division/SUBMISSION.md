# Paper 07: What Men Do with a Day Off

**Target journal:** *Journal of Time Use Research*. It is the field journal for diary-based designs and for timing and synchronisation questions (Hamermesh 2002; Jenkins and Osberg 2005).
**Fallbacks:** *Journal of Population Economics*, *Review of Economics of the Household*, *Feminist Economics*.

## Contribution in one line

The diary day, assigned by the survey's visit schedule and tested for balance within district and quarter, identifies how much of the paid-work time regular salaried men give up on Sundays goes into unpaid work rather than leisure, and how their wives' time and the within-couple division respond, with placebo groups, a falsification test on men without paid work, Saturday as a second instrument and heterogeneity by children and location.

## Cover letter (draft)

> Dear Editor,
>
> I submit "What Men Do with a Day Off" for consideration in *Journal of Time Use Research*.
>
> If men did more unpaid work when they worked fewer paid hours, shorter working time would be a lever for gender equality at home. India's Time Use Survey 2024 records each diary for the day before the interview, so the diary day is set by the investigator's visit schedule; I test balance on the pre-determined characteristics the file records, within district and quarter. For regular salaried men, a Sunday sharply reduces paid work. I use this to estimate how much of the time freed from paid work goes into unpaid work, leisure and sleep, and how their wives' time responds.
>
> The manuscript is original and not under review elsewhere. Replication code is public; the microdata are available from the National Statistics Office, Government of India.
