Dear Editor,

I submit "What Men Do with a Day Off: Sundays, Paid Work, and the Gendered Division of Unpaid Work in India" for consideration in the *Journal of Time Use Research*.

The paper uses the day of the week of the diary in India's Time Use Survey 2024 as an instrument for men's paid work. For regular salaried men, a Sunday diary reduces paid work by 197 minutes (first-stage F ≈ 975). Assuming that Sunday affects unpaid work only through paid work, of every 100 minutes of paid work forgone, about 16 go to unpaid domestic and care work, 58 to free time and 23 to sleep. The estimate ranges from 15.9 to 17.6 across eight specifications, including FSU fixed effects. Men without paid work show no significant Sunday change in unpaid work; allowing for the largest direct Sunday effect consistent with that placebo lowers the unpaid share to about 12 per 100, so I present the estimates as the reallocation of men's time on a day off. The within-couple gap narrows on Sundays because husbands do more, not because wives do less. I report that balance on pre-determined characteristics is rejected and show that this does not move the estimates.
