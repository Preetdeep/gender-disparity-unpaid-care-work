India's Periodic Labour Force Survey shows the worker-population ratio of women aged 15 and over rising from 22.0 per cent in 2017–18 to 40.3 per cent in 2023–24. Using the unit-level data for both years, I reproduce the published estimates and decompose this 18.4-point rise. Own-account work and unpaid help in household enterprises account for 86 per cent of it, agriculture for 73 per cent and women working only in a subsidiary capacity for 48 per cent. Changes in the composition of the population by age, education, marital status, sector and state explain none of it. Weekly hours worked per woman rose by 40 per cent, half as fast as the ratio. In 2023–24, 42 per cent of self-employed women in primary production produced only or mainly for household consumption. The rise is concentrated in the forms of work closest to the boundary of what surveys count as work.
