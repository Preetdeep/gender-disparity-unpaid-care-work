India’s Periodic Labour Force Survey (PLFS), the official national labor force survey, shows the worker-population ratio of women aged 15 and over rising from 22.0 percent in 2017–18 to 40.3 percent in 2023–24, a near doubling under unchanged definitions of work. Using unit-level data from the two nationally representative survey rounds (163,000 and 160,042 women aged 15 and over), I reproduce the published estimates and decompose the rise with exact additive accounting, design-based standard errors, and a Kitagawa shift-share decomposition. Consistent with earlier studies, own-account work and unpaid help account for 86 percent of the 18.4-point rise and agriculture for 73 percent. The new results concern measurement. Women whose principal activity was outside the labor force, mostly domestic duties, and who were counted as workers only through a subsidiary activity account for 48 percent of the rise. Weekly hours worked per woman rose by 40 percent, against 84 percent for the worker-population ratio. In 2023–24, 42 percent of self-employed women in primary production produced only or mainly for household consumption; excluding such output, the rule closest to the 2013 international definition of employment, puts the usual-status ratio at 31.5 percent. The rise is concentrated in the forms of work that are most sensitive to how surveys probe. For India, and for other countries adopting the 2013 standards, headline employment rates are best read alongside principal-status, hours-based, and own-use-adjusted measures.
