# Paper 01: Machines, Maids, and Money

**Target journal:** *World Development*. It publishes empirical development work on gender, household production and energy access. Its readers include the clean-cooking and Ujjwala policy community.
**Fallbacks:** *Feminist Economics*, *Journal of Development Studies*, *Energy Policy* (if the paper is reframed around LPG).

## Contribution in one line

This is the first diary-based, task-level test of whether clean fuel, outsourced laundry and sweeping, and live-in help reduce women's time in the specific task each one replaces, using the TUS 2024 household technology questions.

## Cover letter (draft)

> Dear Editor,
>
> I submit "Machines, Maids, and Money: Does Outsourcing Household Production Reduce Women's Domestic Work in India?" for consideration in *World Development*.
>
> India's Time Use Survey 2024 records, for every household, the cooking fuel used and whether laundry and sweeping are done by hand, by machine or by outsourcing. It links these records to a full-day diary for every member. I use this to test the claim behind large clean-cooking and appliance programmes: that technology releases women's time. The design exploits task specificity. Each technology should reduce its own task and not others, so selection on affluence or tastes would show up as uniform effects across tasks. A 1,440-minute accounting then shows where released time goes: to paid work, to other domestic work, or to rest and leisure.
>
> The paper speaks to the development literature on female labour supply in India, to the evaluation of Ujjwala and other clean-cooking schemes, and to the long debate, from Cowan's *More Work for Mother* to Greenwood et al.'s *Engines of Liberation*, on whether household technology changes the gender division of work.
>
> The manuscript is not under review elsewhere. Replication code is public; the microdata are available from the National Statistics Office.
>
> Sincerely,
> Preet Deep Singh
