## Contribution in one line

One time-budget decomposition of the married versus never-married difference replaces separate "penalties" for housework, sleep, social life and exercise. It adds within-couple gaps by the wife's employment and relative education.

## Cover letter (draft)

> Dear Editor,
>
> I submit "Where Do the Minutes Come From?" for consideration in *Journal of Marriage and Family*.
>
> Studies of marriage and time use usually examine one outcome at a time: housework, sleep, social life or exercise. Because a day has exactly 1,440 minutes, the gains and losses must add to zero. Using India's Time Use Survey 2024, I decompose the married versus never-married difference for women and men aged 20 to 39 across all activity divisions. I add within-couple comparisons by the wife's employment and relative education, and a parallel analysis of widowhood.
>
> The manuscript is original and not under review elsewhere. Replication code is public; the microdata are available from the National Statistics Office, Government of India.
