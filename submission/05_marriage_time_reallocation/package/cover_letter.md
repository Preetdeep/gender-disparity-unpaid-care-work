Dear Editor,

I submit "Married and Never-Married Days: Marriage, Gender and the Full Time Budget in India" for consideration as a full-length article in the *Journal of Marriage and Family*.

A day has 1,440 minutes, so a difference in one activity must be matched by differences in others. Using every diary in India's Time Use Survey 2024 (450,457 person-days), I decompose the married versus never-married difference across all nine activity divisions for women and men aged 20–39. Married women report about 280 more minutes a day of unpaid domestic and care work; for men the difference is 26 minutes. The estimate is robust to restricting to ages 20–26 (267 minutes), to reweighting (233 minutes) and to bias adjustment (breakdown δ ≈ 18). In 106,858 linked couples, wives do 91% of the couple's unpaid work. Among women with no child under six the difference is smaller (about 187 minutes), so part of it reflects motherhood. The comparison is descriptive: the survey is a single cross-section, and I do not interpret the estimates as causal effects of marriage.

Companion papers: this manuscript is one of four papers I am submitting from the India Time Use Survey 2024 microdata, each to a different journal and each on a distinct question: a paper on who provides care in households with a member who needs care (submitted to *Feminist Economics*); a paper comparing the time use of sisters and brothers (submitted to the *Journal of Development Studies*); a paper on how salaried men use the time released on Sundays (submitted to the *Journal of Time Use Research*). The papers share the data-processing code and the description of the survey, but not their research questions, samples, estimates or conclusions. I am happy to provide the companion manuscripts to the editor on request.

Prior preprint: an earlier, shorter working paper on a related question, "Marriage Increases Women's Unpaid Care Work by 44%, Men's Unchanged: Evidence from India" (SSRN, abstract 5677063, posted 4 December 2025, revised 12 April 2026; https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=5677063), uses the same survey to estimate the association between marriage and participation in unpaid care work. The present manuscript is a new analysis with a different design and scope: it decomposes the full 1,440-minute day across all nine activity divisions, uses a different sample and comparison group, adds the linked-couple, co-residence and selection analyses, and reports different estimates. The preprint is not peer-reviewed or published in a journal.
