Dear Editor,

I submit "Married and Never-Married Days: Marriage, Gender and the Full Time Budget in India, 2024" for consideration as a Research Article in Demographic Research. I understand that Demographic Research charges no fees and is platinum open access, and the manuscript is submitted exclusively to the journal.

A day has 1,440 minutes, so a difference in one activity must be matched by differences in others. Using all 450,457 diaries of India's Time Use Survey 2024, I compare never-married and currently married women (88,337) and men (87,364) aged 20 to 39 across all nine activity divisions, so that the components of the comparison sum to zero. Married women report 279.7 minutes more unpaid domestic and care work per day than never-married women (95% CI [274.8, 284.6]); for men the difference is 26.2 minutes (95% CI [24.4, 28.1]). In 106,858 linked couples, wives account for 91% of the couple's unpaid work. The difference is smaller among women with no child under six, so part of it accompanies motherhood rather than marriage alone. The data are a single cross-section, so the comparison is descriptive and I do not interpret the estimates as causal effects of marriage. Co-residence with parents-in-law makes no detectable difference to the average unpaid work of married women.

The contribution to the demographic literature is a single time-budget accounting of the married and never-married difference, combined with selection checks (an age restriction, reweighting and bias adjustment), residence-type comparisons that address patrilocality, and spouses' linked diaries. It takes the place of separate estimates of marriage "penalties" for single outcomes.

Previous work and overlap. An earlier, shorter working paper of mine on a related question, "Marriage Increases Women's Unpaid Care Work by 44%, Men's Unchanged: Evidence from India" (SSRN abstract 5677063), uses the same survey to study the association between marriage and unpaid care work. It is a preprint that has not been peer reviewed or published in a journal. The present manuscript is a new analysis with a different design and scope: it decomposes the whole day across all nine activity divisions, uses a different sample and comparison, and adds the linked-couple, co-residence and selection analyses. I disclose the preprint here and not in the manuscript so that the review stays blind.

I have other papers based on the same survey. Each asks a different question, with different samples, estimates and conclusions, and none duplicates this manuscript. I can provide any of them to the editor on request.

The manuscript is original, has not been published in a journal and is not under consideration elsewhere. I have no competing interests and received no funding. I verified all analyses, results, references and text myself and take full responsibility for the content.
