Dear Editor,

I submit "Where Do the Minutes Come From? Marriage and the Reallocation of Women's and Men's Days in India" for consideration as a full-length article in the *Journal of Marriage and Family*.

A day has 1,440 minutes, so the time marriage adds to one activity must come from others. Using every diary in India's Time Use Survey 2024 (450,457 person-days), I decompose the married versus never-married difference across all nine activity divisions for women and men aged 20–39. Married women report about 280 more minutes a day of unpaid domestic and care work; for men the difference is 26 minutes. The estimate is robust to restricting to ages 20–26 (267 minutes), to reweighting (233 minutes) and to bias adjustment (breakdown δ ≈ 18). In 106,858 linked couples, wives do 91% of the couple's unpaid work.

Companion papers: this manuscript is one of four papers I am submitting from the India Time Use Survey 2024 microdata, each to a different journal and each on a distinct question: a paper on who provides care in households with a member who needs care (submitted to *Feminist Economics*); a paper comparing the time use of sisters and brothers (submitted to the *Journal of Development Studies*); a paper on how salaried men use the time released on Sundays (submitted to the *Journal of Time Use Research*). The papers share the data-processing code and the description of the survey, but not their research questions, samples, estimates or conclusions. I am happy to provide the companion manuscripts to the editor on request.
