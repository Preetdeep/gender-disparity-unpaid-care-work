Dear Editor,

I submit "More Paid Work, the Same Unpaid Load: Gender Gaps in Time Use in India, 2019–2024" for consideration as a regular research article in World Development. The manuscript is submitted exclusively to the journal.

India's official labour force statistics record a large rise in women's employment since 2019. The paper asks what that rise meant for how women and men spend the day. It compares India's two nationally representative Time Use Surveys, conducted in 2019 and 2024 (770,848 diaries of persons aged 15 and over), after harmonising the activity classification, state boundaries and household measures, and documents what can and cannot be compared between the two rounds.

The main findings are as follows. Women's mean time in employment rose by 11.1 minutes a day (SE 1.3), almost entirely because more women worked on the diary day (20.6 to 23.7 percent); minutes per worker did not change. Most of the rise reflects a shift of women out of domestic duties and into self-employment and regular salaried work. Women's unpaid domestic and care work did not fall: less time cleaning was offset by more childcare, and the gender gap in unpaid work narrowed by only 3.2 minutes, to 289 minutes a day. Reweighting the 2019 population to the 2024 composition enlarges rather than removes the rise in women's paid work. The paper is descriptive and does not attribute the changes to any single cause or policy.

I believe the paper fits World Development's interest in gender, work and development. It adds to the debate on women's employment in India a whole-day accounting of the change, and the finding that new paid work has so far been added to women's days rather than redistributing their unpaid work bears on employment and care policy in many developing countries.
