Dear Editors,

I submit "More Paid Work, the Same Unpaid Load: Gender Gaps in Time Use in India, 2019–2024" for consideration as a Research Article in Population and Development Review. The manuscript is submitted exclusively to the journal and is not under consideration elsewhere.

India's official labour force statistics record a large rise in women's employment since 2019. The paper asks what that rise meant for how women and men spend the day. It compares India's two nationally representative Time Use Surveys, conducted in 2019 and 2024 (770,848 diaries of persons aged 15 and over), after harmonising the activity classification, state boundaries and household measures, and it sets out what can and cannot be compared between the two rounds.

Women's mean time in employment rose by 11.1 minutes a day (SE 1.3), almost entirely because more women worked on the diary day (20.6 to 23.7 percent); minutes per worker did not change. Most of the rise reflects a shift of women out of domestic duties and into self-employment and regular salaried work. Women's unpaid domestic and care work did not fall: less time cleaning was offset by more childcare, and the gender gap in unpaid work narrowed by only 3.2 minutes, to 289 minutes a day. Reweighting the 2019 population to the 2024 composition enlarges rather than removes the rise in women's paid work. The paper is descriptive and does not attribute the changes to any single cause or policy.

I believe the paper suits Population and Development Review's interest in the relationship between population and social and economic change and in new statistics and their interpretation. It brings a whole-day, time-budget view to one of the most discussed changes in women's work in a large developing country, and its central finding, that new paid work has so far been added to women's days rather than redistributing their unpaid work, bears on how the rise in women's employment should be read.
