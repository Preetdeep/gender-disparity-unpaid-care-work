# Paper 08: The Free-Time Gap over the Life Course (Gender, Family Stage, and Time Poverty in India)

**Target journal:** *Social Indicators Research*. It publishes measures of well-being, leisure inequality and time poverty, including descriptive time-use work.
**Fallbacks:** *Journal of Time Use Research*, *Feminist Economics*, *Asian Population Studies*.

## Contribution in one line

The gender gap in free time and time poverty is traced across seven family stages, decomposed (Kitagawa) into within-stage and stage-composition components, and compared with its conditional dispersion across education, consumption, caste, religion and sector. Time poverty is measured three ways (50%/60% relative free-time lines with FGT1, a long-hours line, a joint time x consumption measure); every main result is reported with and without religious practice; retirement (pensioners vs workers 55-70) and commuting among the employed are analysed. Consolidates twelve one-dimension legacy papers.

> Dear Editor,

> I submit "The Free-Time Gap over the Life Course" for consideration in *Social Indicators Research*.
>
> Gender gaps in free time are usually reported as one national average. Using every diary in India's Time Use Survey 2024, I trace the gap and the incidence of time poverty across seven family stages, decompose the national gap into within-stage and composition components, and compare life-course variation with variation by education, consumption, caste and religion. Time poverty is measured under several lines and jointly with consumption poverty, and results are reported with and without religious practice. I also examine retirement and commuting.
>
