# Paper 08: The Free-Time Gap over the Life Course (Gender, Family Stage, and Time Poverty in India)

**Target journal:** *Social Indicators Research*. It publishes measures of well-being, leisure inequality and time poverty, including descriptive time-use work.
**Fallbacks:** *Journal of Time Use Research*, *Feminist Economics*, *Asian Population Studies*.

## Contribution in one line

The gender gap in free time and time poverty is traced across seven family stages, decomposed (Kitagawa) into within-stage and stage-composition components, and compared with its conditional dispersion (noise-corrected) across socioeconomic position and location: own education, the household's highest education, another member in regular salaried employment, own usual activity status, sector and state. Time poverty is measured with 50%/60% relative free-time lines with FGT1 and a long-hours line, and tabulated by household education, regular salaried employment and sector; every main result is reported with and without religious practice; retirement (pensioners vs workers 55-70) and commuting among the employed are analysed.

## Data constraint (2024 person-level file)

The file has no household questionnaire (Block 4). Not estimable in this paper, and said so in the Data and Limitations sections: the joint time x consumption poverty measure, consumption gradients, and any heterogeneity by caste (social group) or religion. Replacements: roster-based SES proxies (`hh_max_edu_tier`; regular salaried employment of a member OTHER than the person, built locally as `reg_oth`; own `pas_group`), sector, state, and district + day-of-week (+ sub-round where present) fixed effects. `data_disclosure(V)` is in the Data section. If the household-level file becomes available, restore the time x consumption cross and caste/religion heterogeneity.

> Dear Editor,
>
> I submit "The Free-Time Gap over the Life Course" for consideration in *Social Indicators Research*.
>
> Gender gaps in free time are usually reported as one national average. Using every diary in India's Time Use Survey 2024, I trace the gap and the incidence of time poverty across seven family stages, decompose the national gap into within-stage and composition components, and compare life-course variation with variation by education, household socioeconomic position, employment status, sector and state. Time poverty is measured under several lines and by household socioeconomic position, and results are reported with and without religious practice. The unit-level file does not record consumption, caste or religion; the manuscript states what this rules out. I also examine retirement and commuting.
